Curatorial Manifesto

CINEMA IS
ALWAYS NEW.

Every generation has had to reinvent cinema.
We want to see you in your film.

We champion short films with bold artistic vision, a distinct cinematic language and a strong auteur voice. We welcome filmmakers from different backgrounds, working with any budget, any camera and any story that needs to be told.

Read the full curatorial manifesto

From Soviet Montage to Italian Neorealism, the French New Wave and Dogme 95, cinema has repeatedly changed when filmmakers challenged inherited rules. London Nova is interested in that same impulse: not imitation, but discovery.

A small budget is not a small idea. Limitations can create rhythm, texture, urgency and new ways of seeing. We welcome film-school graduates, self-taught filmmakers, first-time directors, experienced artists and creators working across disciplines.

New technology does not automatically create new cinema. What matters is what filmmakers do with it. We do not claim to know the new cinematic language of the 21st century. London Nova exists to look for it.

Our first edition is built around a real London cinema screening, filmmaker presence where possible and audience conversation. The film becomes a conversation.

Competition Categories

SIX WAYS TO
REIMAGINE CINEMA.

Rather than dividing films by conventional genre, London Nova's inaugural competition is organised around six questions: how cinema is changing its relationship with humanity, story, form, reality, authorship and the audience.

01

AI & Humanity

Films exploring the evolving relationship between artificial intelligence and human experience — identity, memory, labour, intimacy, consciousness and the future. AI may be a subject, a creative method, or both; technology alone is not the criterion.

What does it mean to be human now?
02

Unbound Narratives

Work that challenges inherited ideas of storytelling through non-linear, fragmented, marginal, unconventional or deliberately non-traditional narrative structures and perspectives.

How else can stories be told?
03

Beyond the Frame

Moving-image work that pushes at the boundaries of cinema through mixed media, animation and live action, screen-based forms, game engines, found footage, expanded cinema or other cross-disciplinary approaches.

Where does cinema end?
04

New Non-Fiction

Documentary and non-fiction work that finds distinctive ways to encounter reality — including essay film, personal documentary, archival practice, docu-fiction, hybrid and experimental non-fiction.

How can cinema encounter reality?
05

Singular Voices

Highly personal, author-driven films with a point of view that feels unmistakably their own. We value specificity, risk and personality over scale, polish or conventional perfection.

Could this film have been made by anyone else?
06

Interactive Cinema

Works in which the audience becomes part of the cinematic experience through choice, participation, branching narrative, real-time systems, game-film hybrids or other interactive structures.

What if the audience becomes part of the film?
